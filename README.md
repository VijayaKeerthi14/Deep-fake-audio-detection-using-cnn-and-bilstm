# Deep-fake-audio-detection-using-cnn-and-bilstm
The rise of deepfake audio, driven by advanced generative models such as WaveNet, Voice Conversion, and Text-to-Speech (TTS) synthesis, presents significant challenges to digital security, media integrity, and forensic investigations. This study proposes a hybrid deep learning framework that integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) networks to detect synthetic speech. By analyzing Mel-Spectrograms and Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), the model effectively captures both spectral and temporal patterns, distinguishing real and manipulated audio with high accuracy.

A balanced dataset of 94,734 real and fake audio samples was used for training and evaluation, incorporating preprocessing techniques such as noise reduction, feature scaling, and data augmentation to enhance robustness. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CNN-BiLSTM architecture achieves 98% accuracy, outperforming baseline models such as standalone CNNs and LSTMs. Comparative analysis across various deepfake generation techniques confirms the model’s adaptability to different synthesis methods.

This research highlights the potential of hybrid deep learning architectures in safeguarding voice-based authentication systems and mitigating the risks of audio forgery. Future work aims to integrate self-supervised learning for improved generalization, enable real-time detection on edge devices, and enhance model interpretability using attention mechanisms. The proposed system offers a promising step toward securing digital audio against deepfake threats in cybersecurity, media, and forensic applications.
